### 6. Step notes

**Step 1.** Deliver your 15-minute presentation to the steering committee panel.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 2.** Hold the two-minute rule under pressure — lead with outcome, benefit and ask.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 3.** Take questions. Expect challenge on sample adequacy: was your sample size justified, and what power did your tests have?

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 4.** Expect challenge on the measurement system: how do you know the data is trustworthy? Have your Gage R&R or Kappa result ready.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 5.** Expect challenge on assumptions: did you test normality, equal variance and independence, and what did you do when they failed?

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 6.** Expect challenge on confounding: what else changed during the period, and how do you know your intervention caused the improvement?

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 7.** Expect challenge on DOE: what was your design resolution, what effects were aliased, and did confirmation runs validate the optimum?

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 8.** Expect challenge on sustainability: what stops this from decaying in six months once attention moves elsewhere?

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 9.** Expect challenge on the benefit: is it hard or soft, who in Finance validated it, and over what period?

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 10.** Answer with evidence, not assertion. Go to your appendix. Where you do not know, say so and state how you would find out — this is far stronger than bluffing.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 11.** Where a challenge reveals a genuine weakness, acknowledge it and state the mitigation. Defensiveness costs more credibility than the weakness itself.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 12.** Observe the other presentations and record challenges you had not anticipated for your own project.
