## The Scenario

Meridian Medical Devices manufactures infusion pump assemblies across three plants (Singapore, Penang, Suzhou) on six production lines, supplying hospital distributors in 14 countries. The business is missing its on-time-in-full (OTIF) target, first-pass yield varies significantly between plants, and warranty returns are rising. The VP of Operations has appointed you as Black Belt to lead a portfolio of improvement projects — and to mentor the Green Belts running workstreams beneath you. Use this scenario ONLY if you cannot use a real process from your own workplace; your own process is always preferred.

## Step-by-Step

1. Recap brainstorming facilitation in full: divergence before convergence, no criticism during generation, and how to stop the highest-paid opinion from dominating.
2. Recap the fishbone structure: the effect at the head, and 5M+E bones — Manpower, Method, Machine, Material, Measurement, Environment.
3. Write your capstone problem statement precisely at the head of the fishbone. A vague effect produces vague causes.
4. Facilitate the fishbone with your team, generating at least 25 candidate causes across all six bones. Thin bones signal blind spots, not absence of causes.
5. Recap the 5 Whys and its discipline: continue until the answer is a process or system failure, never a person to blame.
6. Apply 5 Whys to your top five fishbone causes. Note where a single chain branches — real causal chains are often trees, not lines.
7. Recap multi-voting and apply it to reduce your cause list to the ten most plausible candidates.
8. Build a cause-and-effect matrix: score each candidate cause against each CTQ, weighted by CTQ importance, to produce a ranked cause list.
9. Filter the ranked list for TESTABILITY — for each cause, can you state a hypothesis and obtain data? An untestable cause cannot be proven in Analyze.
10. Convert your top five testable causes into formal hypothesis statements ready for Lab 17.
11. Note how you would coach a Green Belt team through this session, and the two facilitation mistakes you would watch for.
