### 6. Step notes

**Step 1.** Understand why OFAT fails: it cannot detect interactions at all, and it requires more runs for less information than a factorial design.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 2.** Recap the DOE vocabulary in full: factor, level, response, run, effect, interaction, replication, randomisation, blocking and experimental error.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 3.** Select 3 factors from your capstone that you can actually control and vary — temperature, speed, pressure, batch size, staffing level or method variant.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 4.** Set two levels for each factor — low (-1) and high (+1). Set them wide enough to produce a detectable effect but within safe operating limits.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 5.** Build the 2^3 design matrix: 8 runs covering every combination of the three factors, in standard order.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 6.** Decide replication. Replicates give an estimate of pure experimental error and let you test effect significance — 2 replicates gives 16 runs.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 7.** RANDOMISE the run order. Randomisation protects against unknown time-varying nuisance factors and is not optional.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 8.** Use blocking if runs must span shifts, days or material lots — block on the nuisance factor so it does not contaminate the effects.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 9.** Execute the experiment, holding all non-experimental factors constant and recording anything unexpected during each run.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 10.** Calculate main effects: the average response at the high level minus the average at the low level, for each factor.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 11.** Calculate interaction effects for AB, AC, BC and the three-way ABC.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 12.** Plot main effects (steeper slope means larger effect) and interaction plots (non-parallel lines mean interaction present).

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 13.** Test effect significance using a Pareto of effects or a normal probability plot of effects — points off the line are real effects.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 14.** Build the prediction equation from the significant effects and identify the factor settings that optimise your Y.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 15.** Run confirmation trials at the predicted optimum. If the confirmation fails, the model is missing something — do not roll out.

____________________________________________________________________________________

### 7. Self-check

Your design is fully randomised with replication, you have computed and plotted both main effects and interactions, effect significance is assessed statistically, and confirmation runs validate the predicted optimum.
