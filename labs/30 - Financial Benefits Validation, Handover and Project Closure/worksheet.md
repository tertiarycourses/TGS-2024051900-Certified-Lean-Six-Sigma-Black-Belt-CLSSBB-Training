### 6. Step notes

**Step 1.** Learn the benefit categories precisely. HARD: cost reduction that appears in the P&L. SOFT: capacity or time released without a headcount or cost change. COST AVOIDANCE: costs not incurred that would have been.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 2.** Understand why the distinction matters: Finance books these differently, and claiming soft benefits as hard is the fastest way to lose credibility with a CFO.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 3.** Quantify your capstone's benefit against the Lab 3 baseline, using post-improvement data over a defensible period, not a single good week.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 4.** Verify the improvement is statistically significant, not a favourable run — apply a hypothesis test comparing post-improvement against baseline.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 5.** Confirm sustained performance with your control chart: the shift must hold for a meaningful period before the benefit can be booked.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 6.** Separate your benefit into hard, soft and cost-avoidance components and state each explicitly.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 7.** Take the calculation to Finance and obtain sign-off. Expect challenge on the baseline period, the attribution and whether other changes could explain the result.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 8.** Prepare your attribution argument: what else changed in the period, and why the improvement is attributable to your intervention rather than to those.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 9.** Build the handover pack: control plan, updated SOPs, training records, control charts, reaction plans and the named process owner.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 10.** Conduct the handover meeting with the process owner. They must accept ownership explicitly — an assumed handover is a failed handover.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 11.** Schedule the sustainability audit at 30, 60 and 90 days post-handover, with a named auditor and defined criteria.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 12.** Run the lessons learned session: what worked, what did not, what you would do differently, and what transfers to the Green Belts you mentor.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 13.** Formally close the project with sponsor sign-off, and archive the full DMAIC storyboard.

____________________________________________________________________________________

**Step 14.** Update the project portfolio from Lab 2 — record the closed project's realised benefit and select the next project.

____________________________________________________________________________________
